Approach

Guarding is a management job. We run it like one.

What happens behind the officer you see: who they are, what they work to, who checks them, and who you call.

01People

Selection and training

An officer’s first shift on your site should not be their first time seeing it. Each stage below must be completed before the next.

  1. 01

    Selection

    Screening, reference and background checks as the law requires, then an interview that tests judgement, not just availability.

  2. 02

    Core induction

    Conduct, report writing, emergency procedures, use of radios and logs, and how to deal with the public under pressure.

  3. 03

    Licensing

    Mandatory licensing and training for the jurisdiction, held and in date before deployment. Confirm regulator and licence types

  4. 04

    Site familiarisation

    Shadow shifts on your site with an experienced officer: routes, systems, key contacts, and what normal looks like there.

  5. 05

    Review & refresh

    Supervisor observations, incident reviews and site changes feed into refresher training.

02Process

Every post has a written brief.

Post instructions are written for your site after the assessment, and you approve them before deployment. They are kept at the post and updated whenever the site changes.

When an officer is unsure, the answer should be in the document. If it isn’t, the document is updated.

03Oversight

Checked on the ground, reported on paper.

The frequency of each check is agreed with you in the contract and depends on the size and risk of the site.

Every shift

Handover

The incoming officer reads the log and is briefed in person. Nothing open is left unexplained.

Scheduled & unannounced

Visits

Supervisors check presence, conduct, logs and patrol records against the post instructions.

Every incident

Reports

Written up during the shift, escalated to your named contacts, and followed up until it is closed.

Periodic

Review

A meeting with you on incidents, findings and site changes, with agreed actions in writing.

Escalation

When something goes wrong, everyone knows who calls whom.

  1. 01

    Officer on post

    Makes the scene safe, calls emergency services if needed, records what happened.

  2. 02

    Site supervisor

    Attends or directs, decides whether to escalate further, starts the incident report.

  3. 03

    Operations manager

    Owns the response for serious incidents and arranges extra cover if needed.

  4. 04

    Your named contact

    Informed in the order and timeframe set out in your post instructions.

See it written for your site.

After a site assessment we’ll send recommended posts and draft post instructions for your review. There’s no obligation to go further.
